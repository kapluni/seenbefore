# CLAUDE.md — I've Seen This Before

## Project Summary

"I've Seen This Before" is a semantic similarity engine and public-facing platform that maps Soviet anti-Zionist propaganda (1960s–1980s) to modern antisemitic and anti-Zionist rhetoric on campuses, social media, and in public discourse. The goal is to make the lineage of today's anti-Zionist language visible and undeniable using NLP/ML.

The project was conceived by Ilya Kaplun, an ML engineering manager at Spotify who emigrated from the USSR in 1991 and experienced antisemitism firsthand as a child.

## Key Context

- The scholarly groundwork connecting Soviet propaganda to modern anti-Zionism has been laid by **Izabella Tabarovsky** (author of "Be a Refusenik," senior advisor at Kennan Institute/Wilson Center). She is the primary potential collaborator — outreach has not been sent yet.
- The foundational Soviet text is **Yuri Ivanov's "Caution: Zionism!"** (1970, Progress Publishers Moscow). Full English PDF is freely available at https://www.marxists.org/subject/jewish/caution-zionism.pdf and on Internet Archive.
- Three additional Soviet pamphlets were downloaded from archive.org and integrated into the corpus (see Soviet Sources below).
- The project uses a **trope taxonomy** of 9 Soviet propaganda categories to classify and match content. The taxonomy is defined in `embedding_pipeline.py` under `TROPE_TAXONOMY`.
- Legitimate criticism of Israeli policy MUST score LOW on the similarity engine. This is a core design requirement for credibility. The calibration view in the visualization explicitly demonstrates this. Current avg: **0.43** (well below 0.55 threshold).

## Architecture Overview

```
Soviet Corpus (seed passages + Ivanov + 3 archive.org pamphlets)
    ↓
Sentence segmentation → OCR cleanup → Propaganda pre-filter → Trope tagging → Embedding (BGE-large)
    ↓
Trope-filtered index (only passages with propaganda tropes, ~1800 passages)
    ↓
Modern Corpus (ISCA + CONAN + ADL H.E.A.T. Map + GoldStandard, ~3900 passages)
    ↓
Embed → Similarity search → Dedup → Diversity enforcement → Quality filter → [Optional: LLM verification]
    ↓
viz_data.json → React frontend (static)
/api/analyze → React frontend (live analyzer)
```

## Key Technical Decisions (from March 2026 session)

### Embedding Model: BGE-large-en-v1.5
- **Switched from** `intfloat/multilingual-e5-large` to `BAAI/bge-large-en-v1.5`
- **Why**: Benchmarked on 10 domain-specific test pairs (5 genuine echoes + 5 negatives). BGE-large had **2x the gap** between good and bad scores (0.21 vs 0.11 for E5). Higher good-pair scores AND lower bad-pair scores.
- **Trade-off**: Lost multilingual capability (E5 handles Russian). Since all current corpus is English translations, this is acceptable. Revisit if adding Russian-language sources.
- BGE-large does NOT require query/passage prefixes (unlike E5's "query:" / "passage:" convention).

### Soviet Corpus Pre-Filtering
- The full Ivanov text and archive.org pamphlets contain ~60% noise: historical narrative, quoted Zionist leaders, statistics, citations.
- **`is_propaganda_chunk()`** in `CorpusProcessor` rejects chunks that are:
  - Mostly quoted material (>50% inside quotation marks)
  - Citation-heavy (3+ reference brackets)
  - Pure historical narrative (3+ dates, no propaganda keywords)
  - Mostly proper nouns/statistics (>40% capitalized/numeric)
- **Trope gate**: Only passages with ≥1 trope label are used for matching in `generate_viz_data.py`. This is the strongest quality signal.
- Result: ~1,800 passages from ~3,400 raw chunks (47% kept).

### Match Quality Pipeline
Embedding similarity alone produces ~50% coherent matches. The other 50% are topically related but make different arguments. The pipeline applies multiple filters:

1. **Near-duplicate modern text dedup** — Normalized matching catches "Jews control the U.S. government" / "The Jews control the US government"
2. **Soviet passage reuse cap** — Max 2-3 reuses per Soviet passage (prevents one passage dominating)
3. **Source diversity** — No single modern source >40% of match slots
4. **Heuristic quality filter** (`is_weak_match()`) — Removes pro-Israel false positives, too-short texts, legitimate criticism
5. **LLM echo verification** (`--verify` flag) — Claude Sonnet rates each match as STRONG_ECHO, WEAK_ECHO, NO_ECHO, or FALSE_POSITIVE. Requires `ANTHROPIC_API_KEY`.
6. **Manual curation** — Final matches are hand-reviewed for genuine rhetorical echoing (not just topical similarity)

### Critical Insight: Seed-First Matching, Not All-vs-All
The biggest lesson: **start from the strongest Soviet quotes, then find modern echoes** — not the other way around. The all-vs-all approach (embed everything → rank by similarity → hope the top matches are good) produced ~50% incoherent results because cosine similarity measures topical overlap, not rhetorical echoing. Two texts can discuss Israel from opposite angles and still score 0.80+.

The working approach:
1. Start with the 15 hand-curated Soviet seed passages (short, punchy, unmistakable propaganda claims)
2. For each seed, retrieve the top-10 closest modern texts from the corpus
3. Human review picks the modern text where the **language is strikingly similar** — not just the topic, but the specific claim, framing, and rhetorical structure
4. Each match gets a written explanation of the specific parallel

This produced 10 matches where any reader immediately sees the echo, vs. the automated pipeline which produced 30+ matches where half required strained explanations.

### Methodology Transparency
The project now includes a methodology disclaimer on the Matches tab: these are structural parallels in argumentation, not proven causal chains. Establishing that modern speakers *learned* these arguments from Soviet sources requires historical evidence beyond NLP. The project demonstrates that the rhetorical patterns are strikingly similar — the question of transmission is for historians.

### OCR Text Cleanup
Archive.org PDF extractions have artifacts:
- Hyphenated line breaks: `charac- teristic` → `characteristic`
- Embedded page numbers: `con100 sequently` → `consequently`
- `***` section markers
- These are cleaned in `_clean_text()` in `CorpusProcessor`

### Calibration Scoring
Legitimate criticism uses trope-aware scoring:
- If a legitimate text has NO trope overlap with its best Soviet match, the raw similarity is dampened by 40%
- This ensures "I disagree with settlement policy" scores ~0.37, not ~0.65
- Word boundary matching in trope classifier prevents false positives (e.g., "media" inside "immediate")

## Trope Taxonomy (9 categories)

1. **ZIONISM_RACISM** — Zionism as inherently racist; apartheid comparisons
2. **ZIONISM_NAZISM** — Zionist-Nazi collaboration; Israel employs Nazi methods
3. **ZIONISM_IMPERIALISM** — Israel as tool of Western imperialism; settler-colonial framing
4. **JEWISH_CONSPIRACY** — Zionist control of media, finance, governments
5. **DELEGITIMIZATION** — Denial of Jewish nationhood; "Zionist entity"
6. **WEAPONIZED_ANTISEMITISM** — Dismissing antisemitism claims as Zionist tricks
7. **DUAL_LOYALTY** — Jews as agents of foreign power
8. **BLOOD_LIBEL** — Accusations of deliberate child killing; dehumanization
9. **ANTI_ZIONISM_PROGRESSIVE** — Anti-Zionism framed as progressive/anti-racist duty

## Tech Stack

- **Embeddings**: sentence-transformers (`BAAI/bge-large-en-v1.5` — selected via benchmark, see above)
- **Backend**: Python 3.14, FastAPI, numpy (pgvector for production)
- **Frontend**: Vite + React, recharts, inline styles. Dev server: `cd frontend && npm run dev`

## Conventions

- Soviet passages use source IDs like `ivanov_1970`, `ivanov_full`, `great_soviet_encyclopedia`, `anti_zionist_committee_1983`, `novosti_1970`, `azc_1983`, `progress_1985`
- Modern passages from datasets include a `source` field: `isca_zenodo`, `isca_classdata_2022_2023`, `isca_huggingface`, `conan_multitarget`, `conan_dialogue`, `adl_heat_map`, `goldstandard_2024`
- Confidence tiers: `high` (≥0.85 + trope overlap), `medium` (≥0.70), `low` (≥0.55), `below` (<0.55)
- Legitimate criticism texts are tracked separately for calibration (8 examples, all score <0.55)
- All colors follow the red (Soviet/THEN) / blue (modern/NOW) convention in the UI
- Trope colors are defined in `DEFAULT_TROPE_COLORS` in the React app and in `generate_viz_data.py` metadata
- Matches include both `sovietText` (trimmed for display) and `sovietTextFull` (complete passage)

## Important Design Decisions

1. **Conservative confidence thresholds** — Better to miss a match than to make a false claim. High confidence requires BOTH high cosine similarity AND trope taxonomy overlap.
2. **Legitimate criticism pathway** — The system explicitly includes non-propagandistic criticism of Israel and verifies it scores LOW. 8 diverse examples (policy, peace, humanitarian, legal, etc.) all score avg 0.43.
3. **Transparent methodology** — All taxonomy definitions, training data, and thresholds should be publicly documented. The code is designed to be open-sourced.
4. **Human-in-the-loop** — Matches are hand-curated before display. The auto-generated candidates have ~50% precision; only manually verified genuine echoes are shown.
5. **Trope taxonomy is the intellectual contribution** — The embedding pipeline is standard ML. The taxonomy mapping Soviet propaganda categories to modern rhetoric is what makes this project unique and credible. It should be validated by domain experts (Tabarovsky) before the public launch.
6. **Embedding similarity is necessary but not sufficient** — Cosine similarity finds topical overlap, not rhetorical echoing. LLM verification (`--verify`) or human review is required to confirm matches are genuine echoes making the same argument, not just discussing the same topic.
7. **Propaganda pre-filtering is essential** — The Ivanov text is ~60% historical narrative, quotes, and statistics. Without `is_propaganda_chunk()` filtering, half the matches are incoherent (matching modern rhetoric to medieval Jewish history or Knesset debate transcripts).
